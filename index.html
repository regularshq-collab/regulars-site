— How it actually goes
01 / The problem Sheet 01 of 07

You are on a job,
not by the phone

Nobody calls a plumber for fun. A call you don't answer is a customer with a problem right now — and a list of other numbers to try.

1 in 4 Calls go unanswered Roughly a quarter of calls to small service businesses go unanswered during working hours. Most owners are on a job.
Most Never leave a voicemail Most callers who reach voicemail hang up without leaving a message. You never learn they called.
Far more Phone beats web forms Phone leads convert to booked jobs at meaningfully higher rates than web form leads for local service work.

These are general industry benchmarks for local service businesses, given as conservative ranges. They are not results Regulars has produced — we are new, and we would rather show you your own numbers than someone else's.

02 / Evidence Sheet 01 of 07

Don't take
our word for it

We are new, so here is somebody else's research instead. Three findings, three universities, none of them ours.

7× Get back to them in an hour Businesses that replied inside the hour won the job about seven times as often as ones that waited a single hour longer. Most took 42 hours.
5–9% Money, from one more star Gain a star on your rating and takings went up 5 to 9 percent. Checked against real tax records, so it is cause and effect — not a hunch.
30% Profit, from losing fewer An auto repair chain held on to 5 percent more of its customers. Its profits went up 30 percent.

SourcesIndependent research

Oldroyd, McElheran & Elkington, “The Short Life of Online Sales Leads”, Harvard Business Review, March 2011 — an audit of 2,241 US companies. hbr.org

Luca, “Reviews, Reputation, and Revenue: The Case of Yelp.com”, Harvard Business School — causal estimate using state tax records. hbs.edu

Reichheld & Sasser, “Zero Defections: Quality Comes to Services”, Harvard Business Review, 1990. hbr.org

Why the star study looked at restaurants: their takings show up in public tax records, which is the only way researchers can prove a rating caused the money rather than just noticing the two moved together. The decision being measured is the same one your customers make — picking a local business off a phone screen, mostly on the stars. The other two studies are not restaurants at all: one covers 2,241 ordinary companies answering enquiries, the other an auto repair chain.

None of this is Regulars' own results. We are new. These are other people's studies, and they are why we built the thing this way — not a promise about your shop.

01
Capture

The call you
didn't get to

A call comes in while your hands are full. About a minute later, that caller gets a text from your number asking what they need. It answers the basic questions, checks your calendar, and books the appointment.

You keep your number. You keep your carrier. Nothing about how customers reach you changes.

02
ReviewPing

The review you
never asked for

The job went well. The customer was happy. Then you drove to the next one and never asked. ReviewPing asks for you, once, shortly after the work is finished, while it is still fresh.

It asks. It does not write reviews, buy them, or hide the bad ones. Your rating goes up because your work is good and somebody finally asked.

How ReviewPing works

03
Comeback

The customer who
quietly stopped

They didn't leave angry. They just moved, got busy, forgot your name. Comeback emails them — a reactivation sequence when they have gone quiet, a follow-up after service, and a short seasonal note each month.

You already paid to win those customers once. This is the cheapest work in your whole business.
